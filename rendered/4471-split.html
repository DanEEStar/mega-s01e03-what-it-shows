parser — the shared parser, month-first

Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser reads every date month-first. 02/03 is written as 2 March and read as 3 February, so the booking lands on another day, often another month. The reports come through the app, by email and through the form, which is why the channel does not separate them.

The fix is in the parser: read the date in the writer’s locale, or refuse a date it cannot place. A guessing parser fails silently and nothing downstream can catch the flip.

hint — the form’s format hint, day-first tenant

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field to tenants who write the day first. Their people type what the hint says, month first, and the form reads the value the tenant’s way. These reports all arrive on the form, from the three booking-v3 tenants.

The fix is not in the parser. Change the hint and the read behind it together, or show the chosen date back in words and let the reporter confirm it.

Why they were merged

Decision 0001 merges on the subject line alone. Twelve subject lines carry all 412 reports, so two causes share twelve subjects and landed in one ticket. The merge rule is still the right rule for a busy morning; the count is what needs a second look. A new decision record can add the one check that would have caught this: a report joins a ticket only if its cause matches, and the cause comes from the tenant, never from the reporter’s browser.

What this changes for the desk

  • Count per cause, not per ticket. #4471 stays the container; each ticket above is what engineering fixes.
  • Read the tenant before the tag. Intake tags come from the browser locale and disagree with the tenant’s cause on a number of reports; the tenant decides.
